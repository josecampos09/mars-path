WELCOME

Welcome to MARS PATH

MARS PATH brings together data from NASA's Mars missions in one layered map. It helps astronauts plan safe routes, find science targets, and know what to expect before they step outside. Every layer has a purpose: to get you there, do the science, and bring you back safely.

MARS OVERVIEW

Mars is our neighbor in the solar system and the fourth planet from the Sun. People call it the “Red Planet” because its soil is covered in reddish dust, much like rust. It is smaller and much colder than Earth, with a thin atmosphere that we can’t breathe, so astronauts will always need a spacesuit outside. Mars still has a lot in common with our planet. It has days almost as long as ours, seasons, mountains, valleys and polar ice caps. Scientists believe that long ago it had rivers and lakes, which is why it’s one of the most exciting places to explore and the next big destination for humans.

      Mars Mission Guide

      Background for the mission: the rover that opened the way, the mountain it is climbing, and why its route matters for the first human Marswalk.

      01

      THE EXPLORER

      Curiosity Rover

      NASA's Curiosity rover is a car-sized robotic laboratory exploring lower Mount Sharp on Mars. Scientists use it to drive to different regions of the mountain, photograph the terrain and study its rocks and soil up close.

      • LandedAugust 2012
      • Landing siteBradbury Landing, Gale Crater
      • MissionMars Science Laboratory
      02

      THE TARGET

      Mount Sharp

      Mount Sharp is a mountain about 5 km tall in the middle of Gale Crater. It is made of stacked rock layers: the lowest layers are the oldest, so climbing the mountain is like reading Mars' history page by page. NASA's map shows a proposed route for Curiosity up the lower part of the mountain, through the regions scientists want to explore in the coming years.

      • HeightAbout 5 km
      • LocationCenter of Gale Crater
      • Key featureLayered rocks, oldest at the bottom
      03

      WHY IT MATTERS

      Scientific Importance

      The proposed route helps scientists choose which areas of Mount Sharp to explore. Each layer can show whether Mars once had water and conditions that could support life. The map combines information from several instruments and spacecraft, so the team knows what to expect before the rover gets there.

      04

      THE PLAN

      Proposed Exploration Route

      NASA's map is a visual guide to Curiosity's proposed path up Mount Sharp. It marks the route already driven, the stops the rover investigated, and the regions scientists plan to explore next. Orbital images from the Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter are the base of the map.

      HOW THIS CONNECTS TO MARS PATH

      Curiosity has spent years mapping this region, which makes it one of the best-known places on Mars. MARS PATH uses that knowledge, together with NASA's maps and data, to help a future astronaut plan a safe walk with high scientific value in the same area.
